# Wind

Wind is moving air. On a boat you care about two things: **true wind** (what the weather is actually doing) and **apparent wind** (true wind plus the wind created by the boat's own motion).

Apparent wind is what the sails feel. As boat speed rises, apparent wind shifts forward and often feels stronger than true wind.

## Why it matters

- Sail trim follows apparent wind, not the forecast.
- Points of sail (close-hauled, beam reach, run) are named relative to the wind.
- Gusts and lulls change heel, speed, and the need to ease or sheet in.

## Quick mental model

Think of a sail as a wing standing on its side. Air flowing over both sides creates lift. The angle of the sail to the apparent wind decides whether you get lift, stall, or just drag.
